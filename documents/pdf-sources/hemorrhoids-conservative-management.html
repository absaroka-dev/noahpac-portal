Conservative Management of Hemorrhoids

A practical home-care plan for adults who have been advised to manage uncomplicated hemorrhoid symptoms conservatively.

PA
First, confirm the cause. Rectal bleeding, a painful lump, itching, and anal pain are not always caused by hemorrhoids. This handout supports a clinician’s treatment plan; it does not diagnose the source of symptoms.

Your daily plan

  1. Make stools soft and easy to passIncrease high-fiber foods. A fiber supplement or stool softener may be helpful if your clinician recommends one. Increase fiber gradually if it causes gas or bloating.
  2. Drink enough fluidWater and other nonalcoholic fluids help fiber work. Follow your clinician’s fluid guidance if you have heart, kidney, or liver disease or another reason to limit fluids.
  3. Change toilet habitsGo when you feel the urge. Avoid straining and do not sit on the toilet longer than needed. Gentle cleaning is less irritating than repeated wiping.
  4. Use warmth for comfortA warm bath or sitz bath several times a day may ease pain and irritation. Keep the area gently clean and dry afterward.
  5. Use short-term symptom relief carefullyOver-the-counter pain relievers or hemorrhoid creams, ointments, or suppositories may help. Follow package directions and check with a clinician or pharmacist if you take blood thinners, have kidney disease, or have other medication restrictions.

Contact your clinic

  • Any new or unexplained rectal bleeding
  • Symptoms are not improving after 1 week of home treatment
  • A lump, prolapse, pain, or bleeding keeps returning
  • An over-the-counter product causes a rash, dryness, or worsening irritation
  • Constipation or diarrhea is persistent or worsening

Seek prompt or urgent care

  • Severe anal pain with rectal bleeding
  • Bleeding with abdominal pain, diarrhea, or fever
  • Sudden severe pain or a very tender, firm anal lump
  • Heavy or ongoing bleeding, faintness, or marked weakness

What conservative care is trying to do

The goal is a soft, formed stool that passes without straining, along with less irritation, swelling, itching, and pain. Dietary and toileting changes remain first-line even when additional treatment is eventually needed.

About topical products

NIDDK advises follow-up if an over-the-counter hemorrhoid product has not relieved symptoms after 1 week or causes side effects. Avoid prolonged unsupervised use, particularly of products containing topical steroids or anesthetics.
